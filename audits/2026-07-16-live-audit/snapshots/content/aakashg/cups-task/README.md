# Cups Task

A computerized implementation of [Levin & Weller's Cups Task](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbdm.2003.09.001) — a behavioral economics tool for measuring risky decision-making under gains and losses.

## What It Does

Participants choose between a guaranteed outcome and a risky gamble across multiple rounds. The task isolates how people make decisions differently when facing potential gains vs. potential losses, controlling for probability and magnitude.

Each trial presents cups (1, 2, 3, or 5) where one cup hides a reward or penalty. Participants decide whether to gamble or take the sure thing. The number of cups changes the probability, letting researchers separate risk tolerance from loss aversion.

## Why It Matters

The Cups Task is one of the standard tools in behavioral decision science for studying:

- **Loss aversion** — do people treat losses differently than equivalent gains?
- **Risk calibration** — can people adjust behavior as probabilities change?
- **Developmental differences** — how risk-taking changes across age groups

This matters for product work because every feature with uncertainty (pricing tiers, freemium limits, trial expirations) triggers the same gain/loss framing effects this task measures.

## References

This implementation is based on the following research (included in the repo):

- Levin, I.P. & Hart, S.S. (2003). Risk preferences in young children: Early evidence of individual differences in reaction to potential gains and losses. *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making*
- Levin, I.P. et al. (2008). Neural correlates of adaptive decision making for risky gains and losses. *Cerebral Cortex*
